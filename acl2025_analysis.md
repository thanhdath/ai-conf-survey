## 1. Overview

The 63rd Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics (ACL 2025), held in Vienna, Austria, set a new record in scale, attracting over 8,000 submissions across its main conference, findings, and industry tracks, with more than 1,800 papers ultimately accepted. The programme committee recognised **four Best Papers**, **two Best Social Impact Papers**, **three Best Resource Papers**, **three Best Theme Papers**, **twenty-six Outstanding Papers**, and **forty-seven Senior Area Chair Highlights**, reflecting the breadth of high-quality contributions.

This analysis focuses on the main conference accepted papers, examining their collective trends, dominant methodological paradigms, and the most impactful research directions shaping the field of Natural Language Processing and Computational Linguistics.

## 4. Trending Techniques

Based on the distribution and awarded status of ACL 2025 main conference papers, seven methodological trends emerge as the most prominent technical innovations shaping the field.

### 4.1 Sparse and Efficient Attention Mechanisms
The dominant technical contribution in the efficiency domain is hardware-aware sparse attention, exemplified by NSA (Native Sparse Attention). Unlike post-hoc sparsification approaches, these methods integrate sparsity natively into the training objective, enabling stable end-to-end optimisation while delivering substantial inference speedups. Hierarchical block-wise sparsity—combining coarse-grained global context with fine-grained local selection—has emerged as the most effective design principle.

### 4.2 Byte-Level and Sub-word-Free Architectures
The Byte Latent Transformer (BLT) challenges the long-standing paradigm of fixed-vocabulary tokenisation by operating on raw bytes grouped into dynamically sized patches. Patch boundaries are determined by next-byte entropy, allocating more computational capacity where data complexity is highest. This approach demonstrates, for the first time at scale, that byte-level architectures can match tokeniser-based performance while offering improved robustness to noisy inputs and orthographic variation.

### 4.3 Compression-Theoretic Analysis of Alignment
A theoretically grounded framework for understanding the fragility of alignment fine-tuning employs information-theoretic and data-compression tools. By modelling the relationship between pre-training data volume and alignment fine-tuning intensity, this approach formalises the concept of "elasticity"—the tendency of aligned models to revert toward their pre-training distribution under subsequent fine-tuning. The framework unifies empirical observations of alignment degradation with rigorous theoretical guarantees.

### 4.4 Inference-Time Representation Engineering
Rather than modifying model weights through costly retraining, inference-time interventions directly transform internal representations at forward-pass time. Cross-lingual interventions (e.g., INCLINE) use learned alignment matrices to project low-resource language representations into high-resource language spaces during decoding, achieving significant performance gains at negligible computational overhead.

### 4.5 Mechanistic Interpretability via Circuit Analysis
Mechanistic interpretability research at ACL 2025 moves beyond probing classifiers toward circuit-level analysis of how specific linguistic computations are implemented within transformer attention heads. Studies such as "Llama See, Llama Do" and "Between Circuits and Chomsky" reveal that identifiable attention circuits govern both contextual entrainment and linguistic dependency processing, and that these circuits can be systematically influenced through pre-pretraining objectives.

### 4.6 LLM-as-Judge Evaluation Frameworks
The growing use of LLMs to replace or augment human evaluation has precipitated a secondary wave of meta-evaluation research. Benchmarks such as JUDGE-BENCH, ContextualJudgeBench, and VerifyBench systematically assess the reliability of LLM evaluators across task types, demonstrating that existing LLM judges exhibit significant biases in contextual, reference-based, and reasoning-heavy evaluation scenarios.

### 4.7 Disaggregated Evaluation and Fairness Measurement
Moving beyond aggregate performance metrics, disaggregated evaluation methods (exemplified by SureMap) estimate model performance across fine-grained demographic subpopulations. Complementary work on "difference awareness" (Fairness through Difference Awareness) challenges the naive application of demographic parity, arguing that some forms of group-differential treatment reflect legitimate contextual distinctions rather than bias.

## 5. Trending Topics

### 5.1 LLM Alignment and Safety (Most Active)
The question of whether current alignment techniques reliably instil intended behaviours remains the most contested topic at ACL 2025. The award-winning paper on alignment resistance provides theoretical grounding for longstanding empirical concerns, while a suite of papers on personalised alignment, safety benchmarking, and on-policy self-correction demonstrates the breadth of active research in this space.

### 5.2 Efficient Inference for Long-Context Models
With commercial LLMs routinely operating over context windows of 100k–1M tokens, the computational cost of full attention has become a practical bottleneck. ACL 2025 features multiple architectural proposals—spanning sparse attention, speculative decoding, and layer dropping—that address this challenge at both the hardware and algorithmic levels.

### 5.3 Evaluation Methodology and LLM-as-Judge
A growing awareness of the limitations of automated evaluation metrics—BLEU, ROUGE, and even perplexity—has driven substantial investment in new evaluation frameworks. The proliferation of judge benchmarks, disaggregated evaluation methods, and capability-aligned scaling metrics signals a field-wide reassessment of what it means to measure NLP progress.

### 5.4 Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
RAG systems remain among the most practically impactful research directions, with ACL 2025 contributions addressing knowledge distillation into small models, agentic retrieval pipelines, noise robustness, and active multi-step retrieval for complex reasoning tasks.

### 5.5 Multilingual Equity and Low-Resource NLP
The persistent performance gap between English and other languages motivates a substantial cluster of resource creation (PARME, IndicSynth), cross-lingual transfer (INCLINE), and evaluation methodology (Beyond N-Grams) papers. A meta-analytic perspective reveals systematic design flaws in existing multilingual benchmarks, suggesting that reported progress may overestimate real-world capability for under-resourced languages.

### 5.6 Hallucination, Fabrication & Research Integrity
The study of model hallucinations has expanded beyond factual question answering to encompass scientific writing, citation generation, and research originality. Papers at ACL 2025 reveal that AI-generated text frequently contains plagiarised concepts and hallucinated citations that evade standard plagiarism detectors, raising fundamental questions about the integrity of AI-assisted research.

### 5.7 Mechanistic Interpretability
Understanding the internal computational mechanisms of transformer models—rather than merely characterising their input-output behaviour—has matured from a niche subfield into a mainstream research priority. ACL 2025 outstanding papers in this domain establish clear links between formal language theory, circuit-level analysis, and the observable linguistic behaviours of pre-trained models.

### 6.1 Efficient Architectures for Long-Context Language Modelling

#### Native Sparse Attention (NSA)

**Yuan et al. (2025). *Native Sparse Attention: Hardware-Aligned and Natively Trainable Sparse Attention.* ACL 2025 Best Paper. arXiv:2502.11089.**

The efficiency of transformer-based language models at long sequence lengths is fundamentally limited by the quadratic complexity of full self-attention. Existing sparse attention variants alleviate this cost post-hoc—by masking or approximating attention weights—but introduce a training-inference mismatch that degrades end-to-end performance. Native Sparse Attention (NSA) addresses this gap through a natively trainable sparse attention mechanism that jointly optimises algorithmic sparsity and hardware utilisation from the outset.

**Methodology.** NSA organises keys and values into temporal blocks and routes each query through three complementary attention paths: (i) *compressed coarse-grained tokens*, which aggregate global contextual information through block-level averaging; (ii) *selectively retained fine-grained tokens*, determined by per-query importance scores that preserve local precision; and (iii) *sliding window tokens*, which maintain continuity with the immediately preceding local context. Specialised CUDA kernels are implemented for each path to maximise Tensor Core utilisation and minimise memory bandwidth overhead, ensuring that the theoretical sparsity translates into practical wallclock speedups.

**Results.** Models pre-trained with NSA match or exceed full-attention baselines across general benchmarks, long-context tasks, and instruction-following evaluations, while achieving **11× speedups** on sequences of length 64,000 tokens and extending feasible context lengths to one million tokens. Crucially, the natively trainable design enables stable end-to-end gradient flow through the sparse pathways, eliminating the performance degradation typically associated with post-hoc sparsification.

**Significance.** NSA establishes that hardware-aware sparse attention can be integrated into the pre-training regime without sacrificing model quality, providing a principled blueprint for the next generation of efficient long-context transformers.

---

#### Byte Latent Transformer (BLT)

**Pagnoni et al. (2025). *Byte Latent Transformer: Patches Scale Better Than Tokens.* ACL 2025 Outstanding Paper. arXiv:2412.09871.**

Fixed-vocabulary tokenisation has been a foundational component of large language models since the advent of byte-pair encoding, but it introduces well-documented pathologies: inconsistent handling of morphological variation, brittleness to character-level perturbations, and unequal computational investment across inputs of varying textual entropy. The Byte Latent Transformer (BLT) proposes a fundamentally different approach, operating on raw bytes grouped into dynamically sized patches without any fixed vocabulary.

**Methodology.** BLT consists of three modules: a *lightweight local encoder* that maps raw bytes into patch-level representations; a *latent transformer* that processes patch sequences with standard full attention; and a *lightweight local decoder* that autoregressively generates byte sequences within each predicted patch. The critical innovation lies in the entropy-based patch segmentation: patch boundaries are placed wherever the entropy of the next-byte distribution exceeds a learned threshold, concentrating compute on informationally complex segments while compressing the representation of predictable byte sequences.

**Results.** BLT achieves flop-controlled parity with Llama 3 at the 8B parameter scale trained on 4T tokens, while consuming up to **50% fewer inference FLOPs** owing to adaptive compute allocation. The architecture exhibits markedly superior robustness to character-level perturbations (random insertions, deletions, substitutions) compared to tokeniser-based models, and demonstrates enhanced phonological and orthographic understanding in structured evaluations.

**Significance.** BLT demonstrates, for the first time at scale, that tokenisation-free architectures can compete with—and in certain dimensions, surpass—their tokenised counterparts. The results call into question the assumption that fixed vocabularies are a necessary inductive bias for high-performance language modelling.

### 6.2 LLM Alignment and Safety

#### Language Models Resist Alignment

**Ji et al. (2025). *Language Models Resist Alignment: Evidence From Data Compression.* ACL 2025 Best Paper. arXiv:2406.06144.**

Despite significant investment in alignment techniques—including reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), constitutional AI, and direct preference optimisation—empirical observations consistently show that aligned models can be rapidly de-aligned through relatively lightweight fine-tuning. This paper provides the first rigorous theoretical account of this phenomenon, grounded in information theory and data compression.

**Methodology.** The authors characterise alignment as a modification to the model's implicit data distribution and employ Kolmogorov complexity and normalised compression distance to formalise the relative influence of pre-training versus alignment data on the model's learned distribution. They define *elasticity* as the property by which a post-alignment model resists changes to its alignment-induced distribution when subjected to further fine-tuning on arbitrary data. Formal proofs establish that the ratio of pre-training data volume to alignment fine-tuning intensity determines the degree of elasticity; as models scale and pre-training corpora grow, elasticity necessarily increases. Empirical validations are conducted across model families (GPT-2, LLaMA, Mistral) at multiple scales, measuring the rate at which alignment degraded under continued fine-tuning on non-aligned data.

**Results.** Across all evaluated model families and sizes, alignment degraded rapidly in the early stages of subsequent fine-tuning, followed by a pronounced slowdown as the model approached the pre-training distribution. The rate of this rebound was found to scale positively with model size and pre-training data volume, confirming the theoretical prediction that larger, better-trained models are more resistant to sustained alignment. The authors further show that this property is not an artefact of any particular alignment algorithm but is a fundamental consequence of the relative scale of pre-training versus alignment data.

**Significance.** These findings have profound implications for AI safety: the larger and more capable a language model becomes, the more resistant it is to alignment attempts, raising concerns about the long-term effectiveness of post-hoc alignment as a safety strategy.

#### Fairness through Difference Awareness

**Wang et al. (2025). *Fairness through Difference Awareness: Measuring Desired Group Discrimination in LLMs.* ACL 2025 Best Paper. arXiv:2502.01926.**

Conventional approaches to algorithmic fairness in language models adopt an anti-discrimination principle analogous to racial colour-blindness: groups should be treated identically regardless of membership. This paper challenges the universality of this principle, arguing that in many practically important settings—medical triage, legal adjudication, targeted social services—treating individuals differently based on group membership is not only permissible but ethically required.

**Methodology.** The authors introduce a taxonomy distinguishing three types of group-differential questions: *descriptive* (empirically verifiable group differences), *normative* (value-laden questions about differential treatment), and *correlational* (statistical associations that may or may not license differential treatment). A benchmark suite of 16,000 questions spanning eight distinct scenarios is constructed to evaluate *difference awareness*—the capacity of a model to treat group members differently when contextually appropriate. Ten large language models are evaluated, including GPT-4o, Gemma-2 9B, and several open-source alternatives.

**Results.** Models that achieve near-ceiling scores on existing fairness benchmarks (BBQ, DiscrimEval) frequently fail to exhibit appropriate difference awareness, scoring below 0.75 on DiffAware and CtxtAware metrics. Crucially, standard bias mitigation strategies—including fine-tuning on balanced datasets and constitutional constraints—systematically reduced difference awareness, demonstrating that fairness as conventionally operationalised can interfere with fairness as normatively required.

**Significance.** This paper reframes the fairness problem in NLP, demonstrating that the field's current de facto standard—demographic parity—is neither necessary nor sufficient for fair treatment in practice. The benchmark provides a concrete evaluation tool for the next generation of fairness-aware LLM development.

### 6.3 Multilingual NLP and Cross-Lingual Transfer

#### INCLINE: Inference-Time Cross-Lingual Intervention

**Wang et al. (2025). *Bridging the Language Gaps in Large Language Models with Inference-Time Cross-Lingual Intervention.* ACL 2025 Outstanding Paper. arXiv:2410.12462.**

Large language models exhibit substantial performance disparities across languages, with non-English languages—particularly those from low-resource typological families—consistently underperforming English. Prior approaches to closing this gap require either costly multilingual pre-training or supervised fine-tuning in each target language. INCLINE proposes a lightweight, training-free alternative that operates purely at inference time.

**Methodology.** INCLINE exploits the observation that the internal representations of low-resource and high-resource languages within a shared multilingual LLM occupy geometrically distinct but linearly related regions of the representation space. For each source (low-resource)–target (high-resource) language pair, INCLINE learns an alignment matrix through a least-squares regression objective on parallel sentence pairs. At inference time, intermediate hidden states produced when processing source-language inputs are projected through the alignment matrix, effectively repositioning them within the high-resource language's representation manifold. This intervention is applied selectively to middle transformer layers, where cross-lingual alignment has been found to be most geometrically tractable.

**Results.** Extensive evaluation across nine downstream benchmarks spanning five language families and five LLM architectures demonstrates that INCLINE consistently improves low-resource language performance relative to strong baselines, including multilingual fine-tuning and cross-lingual prompting. The method is computationally lightweight—requiring only a small number of parallel sentences for alignment matrix estimation—and broadly applicable across model families without architectural modification.

**Significance.** INCLINE establishes inference-time representation engineering as a viable strategy for democratising LLM capabilities across under-resourced languages, with implications for NLP equity and the global accessibility of AI systems.

#### Between Circuits and Chomsky

**Hu et al. (2025). *Between Circuits and Chomsky: Pre-pretraining on Formal Languages Imparts Linguistic Biases.* ACL 2025 Outstanding Paper. arXiv:2502.19249.**

The question of how the choice of pre-training data shapes the linguistic inductive biases of transformer language models has long been of theoretical interest. This paper investigates whether pre-pretraining on formal languages—before exposure to natural language—can instil biases that facilitate the subsequent acquisition of natural language structure, and identifies the conditions under which such transfer occurs.

**Methodology.** The authors define a family of formal languages within the C-RASP programming language, which constrains constructions to those computationally realisable by transformer attention mechanisms of finite depth. Transformers are pre-pretrained on formal languages spanning multiple levels of the Chomsky hierarchy (regular, context-free, mildly context-sensitive) before undergoing standard pre-training on natural language text. Transfer quality is measured as reduction in natural language perplexity and improvement on linguistic generalisation benchmarks, with mechanistic analysis identifying which attention heads learned during pre-pretraining are reused during natural language processing.

**Results.** Pre-pretraining on formal languages with hierarchical dependency structure—those capturing long-distance agreement and constituency relations—results in lower natural language perplexity and superior generalisation compared to pre-pretraining on flat or locally conditioned formal languages. Notably, pre-pretraining on a matched volume of hierarchical formal language outperforms pre-pretraining on an equivalent volume of natural language, suggesting that the structural purity of formal languages facilitates a more efficient extraction of dependency learning signals. Mechanistic analysis reveals that attention heads specialised for hierarchical dependency tracking during formal language pre-pretraining are reused and refined during natural language pre-training, providing direct evidence of structural transfer.

**Significance.** These results bridge the Chomsky hierarchy, transformer circuit theory, and pre-training methodology, suggesting that carefully chosen formal language pre-pretraining curricula could yield models with stronger and more sample-efficient natural language learning capacity.

### 6.4 Mechanistic Interpretability

#### Llama See, Llama Do: Contextual Entrainment

**Niu et al. (2025). *Llama See, Llama Do: A Mechanistic Perspective on Contextual Entrainment and Distraction in LLMs.* ACL 2025 Outstanding Paper. arXiv:2505.09338.**

Contextual biases in language model generation—the tendency to reproduce patterns from the immediate context even when they are irrelevant—have been widely documented as a source of factual errors and reasoning failures. This paper provides the first mechanistic account of this phenomenon, termed *contextual entrainment*, tracing it to a specific computational mechanism in transformer attention.

**Methodology.** The authors conduct controlled experiments in which tokens that have appeared earlier in the prompt context are introduced into generation scenarios where they are semantically irrelevant to the query. Logit lens analysis is applied across transformer layers to track how the probability assigned to context tokens evolves throughout the forward pass. Causal intervention experiments—including targeted activation patching and attention head ablations—are used to identify the specific layers and heads responsible for the entrainment effect.

**Results.** A systematic elevation of logit probabilities for previously seen context tokens is observed across a diverse range of language model families (Llama, Mistral, Falcon) and prompt formats, demonstrating that contextual entrainment is a universal, architecture-level phenomenon rather than a model-specific artefact. The effect is found to be predominantly mediated by a small set of attention heads in early-to-middle layers that function as context-copying mechanisms. Critically, while the entrainment effect is mechanistic in origin, its magnitude is modulated by semantic factors: counterfactual prompts (where the context token is implausible given the query) produce significantly stronger entrainment than factual ones, suggesting that the copying mechanism is partially gated by semantic plausibility.

**Significance.** By identifying the circuit-level basis of contextual entrainment, this paper opens pathways for targeted interventions—whether through training objectives, activation editing, or architectural modifications—to reduce susceptibility to this form of contextual distraction.

### 6.5 LLM Behaviour and Decision-Making

#### A Theory of Response Sampling in LLMs

**Sivaprasad et al. (2025). *A Theory of Response Sampling in LLMs: Part Descriptive and Part Prescriptive.* ACL 2025 Best Paper. arXiv:2402.11005.**

As large language models are increasingly deployed in high-stakes decision-making contexts—medical diagnosis, legal advice, economic policy recommendation—understanding the statistical structure of their generated responses becomes a matter of practical importance. This paper develops a theoretical framework characterising LLM response sampling as a two-component mixture of descriptive (statistical) and prescriptive (normative) tendencies.

**Methodology.** The authors define the *descriptive component* as the component of an LLM's response distribution that tracks the empirical frequency of outcomes in the pre-training corpus, and the *prescriptive component* as the residual deviation from empirical frequency toward an implicit ideal encoded in the model. A formal decomposition is derived that separates these components from the raw output distribution using reference datasets with known empirical frequencies. The framework is applied across multiple domains—public health statistics, economic indicators, demographic distributions—where ground-truth empirical frequencies are available for comparison.

**Results.** The prescriptive component is found to be large, consistent, and domain-agnostic: across all evaluated domains, LLM response distributions systematically deviate from empirical frequencies in the direction of culturally salient ideals (e.g., healthy lifestyles in the public health domain, equitable distributions in the economic domain). When LLMs are used as decision-making agents, this prescriptive bias introduces systematic distortions that can significantly affect expected outcomes, raising concerns about the hidden normative commitments embedded in commercially deployed language models.

**Significance.** The descriptive-prescriptive decomposition provides a principled and practically applicable tool for auditing the normative content of LLM response distributions, with implications for AI governance, regulatory compliance, and the responsible deployment of LLMs in consequential decision-making domains.

### 6.6 Evaluation and Benchmarking

#### Capability Salience Vector

**ACL 2025 Outstanding Paper. arXiv:2506.13216.**

Neural scaling laws—which relate training compute to validation loss—have become indispensable tools for extrapolating model performance and informing resource allocation decisions. However, a fundamental challenge remains: validation loss does not straightforwardly predict performance on downstream tasks, and the factors governing this translation remain poorly understood. The Capability Salience Vector framework addresses this gap.

**Methodology.** The Capability Salience Vector is defined as a per-token importance weighting over the validation loss that reflects the relevance of each token to a target downstream capability. These weights are estimated through a meta-learning procedure that optimises for alignment between the capability-weighted loss and empirical downstream task performance across a diverse set of checkpoint evaluations. The resulting vector provides a fine-grained decomposition of the validation loss signal into capability-aligned components, enabling interpretable scaling law predictions that distinguish, for example, the contribution of mathematical reasoning tokens from natural language understanding tokens.

**Results.** The Capability Salience Vector approach achieves substantially better downstream task prediction accuracy than standard validation loss across a suite of benchmark tasks, including mathematical reasoning, reading comprehension, and code generation. The approach also reveals that different capabilities scale at different rates under increased compute, and that standard validation loss conflates these disparate scaling trajectories.

**Significance.** This framework provides a more granular and predictive connection between training dynamics and downstream capability, potentially transforming how scaling experiments are designed and interpreted.

### 6.7 Biomedical NLP and Global Health

#### AfriMed-QA

**Best Social Impact Paper, ACL 2025. arXiv:2411.15640.**

The deployment of language models in healthcare is predominantly evaluated on benchmarks derived from Western medical education systems, leaving the performance of these models on African medical contexts systematically uncharacterised. AfriMed-QA addresses this lacuna through the construction and evaluation of the first large-scale, pan-African medical question-answering dataset.

**Methodology.** The dataset comprises 15,000 questions—4,000+ multiple-choice questions, 1,200+ short-answer questions, and 10,000 consumer queries—sourced from over 60 medical schools across 16 African countries and covering 32 medical specialties. Evaluation involves both automated metrics (MCQ accuracy) and structured expert clinical review (examining correctness, localisation, omission, hallucination, and harm potential). Thirty language models are evaluated, spanning large and small, open and closed, general and biomedical variants.

**Results.** Performance on AfriMed-QA MCQs lags substantially behind performance on USMLE-derived benchmarks, even for frontier models, suggesting that standard medical AI benchmarks overestimate clinical utility in African health contexts. Biomedical fine-tuned models unexpectedly underperform general-purpose models, potentially reflecting the domain mismatch between African medical curricula and the Western biomedical corpora used in fine-tuning. Consumer preference evaluations reveal that users frequently rate LLM responses as more helpful and thorough than clinician responses, highlighting the risk of over-reliance on AI in under-resourced clinical settings.

**Significance.** AfriMed-QA establishes the African continent as a critical test bed for medically deployed NLP systems and provides the first systematic evidence that frontier LLMs are not ready for unsupervised deployment in African healthcare without significant adaptation.

### 6.8 Hallucination and Research Integrity

#### HalluCitation Matters

**arXiv:2601.18724.**

The adoption of large language models as research assistants has raised concerns that AI-generated scientific text may contain fabricated citations—a form of hallucination with uniquely serious consequences for the integrity of the scientific record. This paper presents the first large-scale systematic investigation of hallucinated citations (*HalluCitations*) in AI-generated academic writing.

**Methodology.** A corpus of papers published at major ACL venues (ACL, NAACL, EMNLP) in 2024 and 2025 is analysed using a combination of automated citation verification (checking whether cited works exist and match their claimed attributes) and expert human review to assess whether cited works support the claims attributed to them. Standard plagiarism detection tools are applied to the same corpus to assess their sensitivity to HalluCitations.

**Results.** The study identifies HalluCitations in nearly 300 published papers, with the majority appearing in 2025, a period coinciding with the rapid proliferation of AI writing assistance tools. Qualitative analysis reveals that a substantial proportion of hallucinated ideas—assessed by domain experts—show partial but undisclosed conceptual overlap with prior published work, suggesting that LLM-assisted writing may produce implicit plagiarism that evades both standard detectors and peer review scrutiny.

**Significance.** HalluCitations pose a distinct threat to the reliability of the scientific literature, differing qualitatively from outright fabrication in that they are often partially grounded in genuine prior work but presented without appropriate attribution. The findings motivate the development of citation-specific verification tools and revisions to journal policies regarding AI-assisted writing.

## Summary and Outlook

ACL 2025 reflects a field in the midst of a structural transition. The dominant research questions are no longer primarily about *whether* large language models can perform linguistic tasks—they demonstrably can—but about *how reliably and safely* they perform them, *how efficiently* they can be deployed, *how equitably* they serve global populations, and *how faithfully* their internal mechanisms can be understood and audited.

The four Best Papers collectively illustrate this transition with remarkable clarity:
- **NSA** addresses the efficiency frontier, demonstrating that scale need not be synonymous with computational profligacy.
- **Language Models Resist Alignment** raises fundamental concerns about the durability of safety measures, suggesting that alignment may be structurally limited by the scale of pre-training.
- **Fairness through Difference Awareness** complicates the relationship between fairness and equality, showing that treating all groups identically can be as unjust as treating them differently.
- **A Theory of Response Sampling** reveals that deployed LLMs embody hidden normative commitments that may systematically bias decisions in high-stakes domains.

Taken together, these contributions signal a maturing field that is increasingly concerned with the second-order consequences of LLM deployment and the theoretical foundations of the techniques it employs.
